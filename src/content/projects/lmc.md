The LMC sits 160 000 light years away and is seen almost face on, so its whole
disk fits in one map. Planck measured the polarized glow of its dust at 353 GHz,
and the orientation of that polarization gives the direction of the magnetic
field across the galaxy.

Atomic hydrogen gives a second view. Turbulent gas is stretched along the field,
so the direction in which velocity changes fastest is perpendicular to it. The
same 21 cm data also tell cold gas from warm gas, which is where stars begin.
